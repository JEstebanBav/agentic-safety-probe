The closest related studies identify refusal directions in conversational models ([Arditi et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717)), benchmark harmful behaviour in tool-using agents ([AgentHarm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09024)), or investigate interventions such as refusal-vector ablation and activation steering ([Lermen et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10871); [Pinto, 2026, v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09156v1)). Our study connects these approaches by testing whether a conversational refusal direction retains its ability to detect harmful intent when identical instructions are presented in an agentic format. Using 96 harmful–benign pairs across four open-weight models, we measure changes in projection, held-out discrimination and directional alignment, while controlling for benign shifts, scale contraction, circular estimation and layer selection. We also examine explicit, contextual and legitimately framed harmful intent, and distinguish diagnostic changes from observed refusal and model-dependent causal effects. Our contribution is a controlled assessment of how refusal-related representations change across formats and whether monitors trained in the agentic format can recover the signal on this corpus.
